# Medical Negligence — Legal Remedies Under Indian Law

> **Disclaimer**: This document is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Consult a qualified advocate before taking legal action.

## Governing Laws

| Statute | Relevance |
|---------|-----------|
| **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)** | Criminal liability — replaced IPC w.e.f. 1 July 2024 |
| **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** | Criminal procedure — replaced CrPC |
| **Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (CPA)** | Civil remedy — medical services are "services" under CPA |
| **Indian Medical Council Act, 1956 / NMC Act, 2019** | Disciplinary action against registered medical practitioners |
| **Clinical Establishments (Registration & Regulation) Act, 2010** | Standards for hospitals/clinics |
| **Constitution of India — Article 21** | Right to life includes right to health and quality medical care |

## What Constitutes Medical Negligence

Medical negligence is established when **all four elements** are proven:

| Element | Test |
|---------|------|
| **1. Duty of Care** | Doctor-patient relationship existed (consultation, admission, treatment) |
| **2. Breach of Duty** | The doctor failed to exercise the standard of care that a reasonably competent doctor in the same field would have exercised (the **Bolam Test** — adopted by Indian courts) |
| **3. Causation** | The breach directly caused the injury/harm (not a pre-existing condition or unrelated event) |
| **4. Damage** | The patient suffered actual harm — physical injury, worsening condition, death, financial loss, mental suffering |

### Examples of Medical Negligence

- Wrong diagnosis leading to incorrect treatment
- Surgical errors (wrong site, retained instruments, nerve damage)
- Failure to obtain informed consent
- Prescription of wrong medication or dosage
- Delay in treatment/referral causing worsening
- Post-operative neglect leading to infection/complications
- Anaesthesia errors
- Failure to conduct necessary tests before surgery

## BNS Provisions (Criminal Remedies)

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 replaced the Indian Penal Code. Key sections for medical negligence:

### Section 106 BNS — Causing Death by Negligence

> *Whoever causes death of any person by doing any rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide, shall be punished...*

| Sub-section | Scenario | Punishment |
|-------------|----------|------------|
| **Section 106(1)** | Death caused by rash or negligent act | Imprisonment up to **5 years** + fine |
| **Section 106(2)** | Death caused by rash/negligent act of a **registered medical practitioner** while performing medical procedure | Imprisonment up to **2 years** + fine. **Arrest only after investigation by a gazetted officer and approval from appropriate authority.** |

> **Key change from IPC**: Section 106(2) BNS provides **specific protection for doctors** — they cannot be arrested immediately. Investigation must be conducted first. This was a major reform to prevent harassment of doctors.

### Section 125 BNS — Act Endangering Life or Personal Safety of Others

> *Whoever does any act so rashly or negligently as to endanger human life or personal safety of others...*

| Provision | Punishment |
|-----------|------------|
| Simple offence | Imprisonment up to **3 months** OR fine up to ₹2,500 OR both |
| With knowledge of likelihood of harm | Imprisonment up to **6 months** OR fine up to ₹5,000 OR both |

### Section 118 BNS — Voluntarily Causing Hurt

Applicable when a doctor intentionally performs a procedure known to cause harm without valid medical justification.

| Provision | Punishment |
|-----------|------------|
| Section 118(1) | Imprisonment up to **1 year** OR fine up to ₹10,000 OR both |

### Section 124 BNS — Causing Hurt by Act Endangering Life

When a medical act done with recklessness causes hurt:

| Provision | Punishment |
|-----------|------------|
| Section 124 | Imprisonment up to **6 months** OR fine up to ₹5,000 OR both |

### Section 105 BNS — Culpable Homicide Not Amounting to Murder

In extreme cases where a doctor knew the act was likely to cause death:

| Provision | Punishment |
|-----------|------------|
| Section 105 | Imprisonment up to **10 years** + fine |

> **Note**: This section applies only in cases of **gross negligence** amounting to reckless disregard for life — not ordinary clinical errors.

## Civil Remedies

### A. Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Medical treatment is a **"service"** under CPA (Supreme Court ruling in **Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha, 1995**). Patients can file complaints for:

- Deficiency in service
- Unfair trade practice (overcharging, unnecessary procedures)

| Forum | Jurisdiction (Compensation Claimed) | Appeal |
|-------|--------------------------------------|--------|
| **District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission** | Up to ₹1 crore | State Commission |
| **State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission** | ₹1 crore – ₹10 crore | National Commission |
| **National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC)** | Above ₹10 crore | Supreme Court |

**Advantages of Consumer Forum**:
- No court fee (free filing up to ₹5 lakh; nominal fee beyond)
- Faster disposal (mandated 3–5 months, practically 1–3 years)
- Simpler procedure than civil courts
- No advocate required (can argue in person)

### B. Civil Court — Tort of Negligence

File a civil suit for damages under general tort law in the appropriate civil court.

| Court | Jurisdiction |
|-------|-------------|
| **City Civil Court / District Court** | Based on claim amount and defendant's location |
| **High Court** | Appeals or original jurisdiction for larger claims |

**Damages claimable**:
- Medical expenses incurred for corrective treatment
- Loss of income / earning capacity
- Pain and suffering (non-pecuniary)
- Future medical costs
- Loss of consortium (for spouse)
- Funeral expenses (in death cases)

### C. Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal (MACT)

If negligence occurred during treatment of accident injuries and compounded the harm, a separate claim before MACT is possible.

## Disciplinary / Regulatory Remedies

### National Medical Commission (NMC) / State Medical Council

File a complaint against the doctor for professional misconduct.

| Outcome | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| Warning | Formal reprimand on record |
| Suspension of license | Doctor cannot practice for specified period |
| Permanent removal from register | Doctor can never practice again |

## Step-by-Step Process for the Victim

### Phase 1: Immediate Actions (Day 1–7)

#### Step 1: Preserve All Evidence

```
[ ] Collect and photocopy ALL medical records:
    - OPD slips / prescriptions
    - Admission records
    - Discharge summary
    - Surgery/procedure notes
    - Consent forms
    - Lab reports, imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT scans)
    - Billing receipts
    - Ambulance records
[ ] Photograph any visible injuries
[ ] Save all text messages / WhatsApp / email communication with the doctor/hospital
[ ] Note names of all treating doctors, nurses, and hospital staff
[ ] Preserve medicine strips / injection vials if applicable
[ ] Get a second medical opinion (in writing) from another qualified doctor
```

> **Legal right**: Under Section 1.3 of the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, patients have the **right to obtain copies of all medical records**. Hospitals cannot refuse.

#### Step 2: Document the Timeline

Create a detailed chronological account:
- Date and time of each consultation / procedure
- What was communicated by the doctor
- What treatment was given
- When symptoms worsened
- When you realized something was wrong

#### Step 3: Obtain Second Expert Opinion

- Consult a **specialist in the same field** as the treating doctor
- Request a **written opinion** stating whether the treatment was below standard of care
- This forms the basis of your medical negligence claim

---

### Phase 2: Legal Consultation (Week 1–2)

#### Step 4: Engage a Lawyer

Hire an advocate experienced in:
- Medical negligence litigation
- Consumer protection cases
- Criminal complaints under BNS

**Questions to ask**:
1. Which forum is best for my case — consumer court, criminal, or both?
2. What is the estimated compensation I can claim?
3. What is the expected timeline?
4. What are the costs?

#### Step 5: Send Legal Notice

Before filing any case, send a **legal notice** to the doctor and hospital:

**Contents of legal notice**:
- Facts of the case (chronological)
- Specific acts of negligence
- Injuries/harm suffered
- Compensation demanded
- Time period for response (typically 15–30 days)
- Statement that legal proceedings will be initiated if no satisfactory response

**Purpose**: Creates a formal record, may lead to out-of-court settlement, and is mandatory before filing consumer complaint (Section 35(1) CPA 2019 allows but does not mandate it; however, it strengthens your case).

---

### Phase 3: Filing the Case (Week 3–8)

You have **multiple parallel remedies** — you can pursue more than one simultaneously.

#### Option A: Criminal Complaint Under BNS

**When to choose**: Death, permanent disability, or gross negligence.

##### Step 6A: File FIR at Police Station

1. Visit the police station with jurisdiction over the hospital location
2. File a written complaint citing:
   - **Section 106(1) or 106(2) BNS** — causing death by negligence
   - **Section 125 BNS** — act endangering life
   - **Section 118/124 BNS** — causing hurt
3. Attach: medical records, second expert opinion, legal notice copy
4. If police refuse to file FIR → proceed to Step 6B

> **Under Section 106(2) BNS**: For registered medical practitioners, the police **cannot arrest immediately**. They must first investigate, and arrest requires approval from the appropriate medical authority. This is a safeguard built into the new law.

##### Step 6B: Private Complaint Before Magistrate (If FIR Refused)

1. File a **private complaint under Section 223 BNSS** (equivalent of old Section 200 CrPC) before the Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC)
2. Magistrate examines complainant on oath
3. If prima facie case exists, Magistrate directs investigation under Section 175(3) BNSS or issues process against the accused

##### Step 6C: Writ Petition in High Court (Section 482 BNSS / Article 226)

If the Magistrate also refuses, approach the High Court under inherent jurisdiction to direct FIR registration.

#### Option B: Consumer Complaint Under CPA 2019

**When to choose**: For compensation — fastest and most cost-effective remedy.

##### Step 7: File Consumer Complaint

1. **Draft the complaint** containing:
   - Parties (complainant and opposite party — doctor + hospital)
   - Facts of the case
   - Specific deficiency in service
   - Expert medical opinion supporting negligence
   - Compensation claimed (itemized)
   - Relief sought

2. **File at the appropriate Consumer Forum** based on compensation amount:
   - Up to ₹1 crore → District Commission
   - ₹1–10 crore → State Commission
   - Above ₹10 crore → NCDRC

3. **Attach supporting documents**:
   - All medical records
   - Expert opinion
   - Bills and receipts
   - Income proof (for loss of earnings claim)
   - Legal notice and response (if any)
   - Death certificate (in fatal cases)
   - Affidavit of the complainant

4. **Filing fee**: Nil up to ₹5 lakh claim; scaled fee up to ₹75,000 for higher claims

5. **E-filing available**: [edaakhil.nic.in](https://edaakhil.nic.in/)

##### Step 8: Attend Hearings

- Opposite party files written response (30 days)
- Evidence stage (both sides present documents + expert testimony)
- Arguments
- **Expected timeline**: 6 months – 3 years (varies by forum backlog)

#### Option C: Civil Suit for Damages

**When to choose**: Complex cases requiring detailed evidence examination; cases where consumer forum jurisdiction is uncertain.

##### Step 9: File Civil Suit

1. File before the competent Civil Court (based on claim amount and territorial jurisdiction)
2. Pay ad valorem court fee (percentage of compensation claimed — varies by state)
3. Longer process: 3–10 years typically
4. More thorough evidence examination, cross-examination of expert witnesses

#### Option D: Complaint to Medical Council

##### Step 10: File Disciplinary Complaint

1. File a written complaint to the **State Medical Council** (for state-registered doctors) or **Ethics and Medical Registration Board** under NMC (for interstate/central doctors)
2. Attach medical records and expert opinion
3. The council conducts an inquiry
4. **Outcome**: Warning, suspension, or removal of medical license
5. This does NOT provide compensation — pursue alongside civil/consumer remedy

---

### Phase 4: Execution and Appeal (Post-judgment)

#### Step 11: Enforce the Order

| Forum | Enforcement |
|-------|-------------|
| **Consumer Commission award** | If not complied within 30 days, file execution petition. Non-compliance is punishable with imprisonment up to **3 years** or fine up to ₹50,000 or both (Section 72 CPA 2019) |
| **Criminal conviction** | Executed by the court directly |
| **Civil court decree** | Execution petition under Order 21 CPC |

#### Step 12: Appeal (If Dissatisfied)

| Original Forum | Appeal To | Time Limit |
|----------------|-----------|------------|
| District Consumer Commission | State Consumer Commission | **45 days** |
| State Consumer Commission | NCDRC | **30 days** |
| NCDRC | Supreme Court | **30 days** |
| Magistrate/Sessions Court | High Court | **30/60 days** |
| Civil Court | High Court | **90 days** |

## Compensation Framework

Indian courts consider the following heads of damages:

| Head | Description | Typical Range |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Medical expenses** | Actual cost of corrective treatment, hospitalization, medicines | Actual receipts |
| **Future medical costs** | Ongoing treatment, rehabilitation, prosthetics | Expert estimate |
| **Loss of income** | Salary/wages lost during treatment + recovery | Actual proof |
| **Loss of earning capacity** | Permanent disability reducing future income | Multiplier method |
| **Pain and suffering** | Non-pecuniary — physical pain, mental agony | ₹1 lakh – ₹50 lakh |
| **Loss of amenities** | Inability to enjoy life as before (e.g., paralysis) | ₹2 lakh – ₹1 crore |
| **Loss of expectation of life** | Reduced lifespan due to negligence | ₹5 lakh – ₹50 lakh |
| **Funeral expenses** | In death cases | Actual + ₹25,000–₹1 lakh |
| **Loss of consortium** | Spouse's loss of companionship | ₹5 lakh – ₹25 lakh |
| **Punitive damages** | To punish gross negligence / deter future negligence | Court's discretion |

## Limitation Periods (Time Limits to File)

| Remedy | Limitation | Statute |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Consumer complaint** | **2 years** from the date the cause of action arose | Section 69(1) CPA 2019 |
| **Criminal complaint** | **3 years** for most BNS offences (cognizable) | Section 468 BNSS |
| **Civil suit** | **3 years** from the date of negligence | Article 72/73/74, Limitation Act 1963 |
| **Medical Council complaint** | No fixed limitation, but file promptly | NMC regulations |

> **Important**: If you discover the negligence later (e.g., retained surgical instrument found years later), limitation runs from the **date of discovery**, not the date of surgery.

## Landmark Judgments to Cite

| Case | Court | Key Principle |
|------|-------|---------------|
| **Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (1995)** | Supreme Court | Medical services fall under Consumer Protection Act |
| **Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005)** | Supreme Court | Simple error of judgment ≠ negligence; Bolam test adopted; no arrest without expert opinion for doctors |
| **Kusum Sharma v. Batra Hospital (2010)** | Supreme Court | Negligence to be judged by standard of reasonable doctor, not highest skilled |
| **Malay Kumar Ganguly v. Dr. Sukumar Mukherjee (2009)** | Supreme Court | ₹1 crore compensation awarded for gross negligence causing death |
| **Dr. Suresh Gupta v. Govt. of NCT Delhi (2004)** | Supreme Court | Distinction between civil and criminal negligence — criminal requires "gross" negligence |
| **Balram Prasad v. Kunal Saha (2014)** | Supreme Court | ₹11.41 crore — highest medical negligence compensation in India (death of wife due to negligent treatment) |
| **Martin F. D'Souza v. Mohd. Ishfaq (2009)** | Supreme Court | Before prosecuting a doctor, consult a competent doctor to establish prima facie negligence |

## Practical Tips

1. **Time is critical** — preserve evidence immediately; hospitals may alter records
2. **Right to medical records is absolute** — if hospital refuses, file RTI (for government hospitals) or consumer complaint for the refusal itself
3. **Get the expert opinion BEFORE filing** — cases without expert medical evidence almost always fail
4. **Consumer forum is usually the best first step** — faster, cheaper, and compensation-focused
5. **Criminal + consumer can run in parallel** — they are independent jurisdictions with different standards of proof
6. **Standard of proof**:
   - Criminal: **Beyond reasonable doubt**
   - Consumer/Civil: **Preponderance of probability** (balance of probabilities — easier to prove)
7. **Medicolegal case (MLC)**: If you suspect negligence during treatment, ask the hospital to register an MLC. If they refuse, go directly to the police
8. **Insurance**: Check if the hospital/doctor has **professional indemnity insurance** — this ensures they can actually pay the compensation awarded
9. **Preserve original documents** — always submit photocopies to courts; retain originals
