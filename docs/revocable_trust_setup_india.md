# Revocable Trust Setup — India

> **Disclaimer**: This document is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal or tax advice. Consult a qualified trust/estate lawyer and chartered accountant before proceeding.

---

## Governing Law

| Statute | Scope |
|---------|-------|
| **Indian Trusts Act, 1882** | Private trusts (your case) — creation, duties, rights |
| **Registration Act, 1908** | Mandatory registration when immovable property is involved |
| **Indian Stamp Act, 1899** (+ state amendments) | Stamp duty on the trust deed |
| **Income Tax Act, 1961** (Sections 60–64, 161–164) | Taxation of revocable trust income |
| **SEBI regulations** | Transfer/holding of securities in trust name |
| **RBI/FEMA** | If any foreign assets or NRI beneficiaries involved |

---

## Key Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Settlor / Author** | You — the person creating and funding the trust |
| **Trustee** | Person(s) or entity managing trust assets per the deed. Can be yourself. |
| **Beneficiary** | Person(s) who benefit from the trust (can include yourself) |
| **Trust Deed** | The legal document that creates and governs the trust |
| **Trust Property / Corpus** | Assets transferred into the trust |
| **Revocable Trust** | A trust the settlor can amend or revoke at any time during their lifetime |

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Define Objectives and Structure

**Timeline: Week 1**

1. **Clarify purpose** — asset protection, succession planning, avoiding probate, tax planning, or all of the above
2. **Decide trust type** — Revocable (you retain full control and can dissolve it)
3. **Identify:**
   - **Settlor**: You
   - **Trustee(s)**: Yourself (as managing trustee) + 1–2 successor trustees (family member, trusted person, or professional trustee company)
   - **Beneficiaries**: Yourself (primary, during lifetime) + family members (successor beneficiaries)
4. **List all assets** to be transferred:
   - Demat stocks (equity holdings)
   - Bonds / debentures / NCDs
   - Mutual fund units
   - Fixed deposits
   - Savings/current account balances
   - Any other liquid wealth

**Deliverable**: A one-page summary of trust structure, parties, and asset inventory.

---

### Step 2: Engage a Trust Lawyer

**Timeline: Week 1–2**

1. Hire a lawyer specializing in **trust and estate law** (not general corporate law)
2. Confirm the lawyer has experience with:
   - Indian Trusts Act, 1882 private trusts
   - SEBI demat transfer procedures
   - Income tax implications under Sections 60–64
3. **Share your objectives and asset list** with the lawyer
4. Discuss:
   - Whether the trust should be **determinable** (specific end conditions) or **perpetual**
   - Powers reserved by the settlor (amendment, revocation, asset management)
   - Successor trustee appointment mechanism
   - Distribution provisions (during lifetime and after death)

**Estimated cost**: ₹25,000 – ₹2,00,000 depending on complexity and city.

---

### Step 3: Draft the Trust Deed

**Timeline: Week 2–4**

The trust deed is the foundational legal document. It must contain:

#### Mandatory Clauses

| Clause | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Parties** | Full names, addresses, Aadhaar/PAN of settlor, trustee(s), beneficiaries |
| **Declaration of Trust** | Clear statement that the settlor creates a trust and transfers the corpus |
| **Trust Name** | e.g., "The [Your Surname] Family Trust" |
| **Trust Property (Schedule)** | Detailed list of all assets being settled into the trust |
| **Revocability Clause** | Explicit statement that the trust is revocable by the settlor at any time |
| **Objects of the Trust** | Purpose — management, preservation, and distribution of assets |
| **Powers of Trustee** | Investment, sale, purchase, management of securities, bank operations |
| **Powers Reserved by Settlor** | Right to amend, revoke, add/remove beneficiaries, direct investments |
| **Distribution Provisions** | How income and corpus are distributed — during lifetime and after death |
| **Successor Trustee** | Who takes over if the primary trustee dies or is incapacitated |
| **Dispute Resolution** | Arbitration clause (avoid courts) |
| **Governing Law** | Indian Trusts Act, 1882; jurisdiction (your city's courts) |

#### Critical Clauses for Financial Assets

```
INVESTMENT POWERS: The Trustee shall have full power to open and operate
demat accounts, bank accounts, and trading accounts in the name of the
Trust; to buy, sell, pledge, or transfer stocks, bonds, mutual funds,
and other securities; and to enter into any financial transaction
necessary for the proper management of Trust assets.

REVOCATION CLAUSE: The Settlor reserves the absolute and unconditional
right to revoke this Trust in whole or in part, at any time, by
executing a written instrument of revocation delivered to the Trustee.
Upon full revocation, all Trust property shall revert to the Settlor.
```

**Deliverable**: Finalized trust deed reviewed and approved by your lawyer.

---

### Step 4: Execute the Trust Deed

**Timeline: Week 4–5**

1. **Print the trust deed** on stamp paper of appropriate value (see Step 5)
2. **Settlor signs** the deed in the presence of **two witnesses**
3. **Trustee(s) sign** accepting their appointment
4. **Witnesses sign** with full name, address, and signature
5. All parties should carry:
   - PAN card
   - Aadhaar card
   - Passport-size photographs
   - Address proof

**Important**: Under Section 5 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882:
- Trust of **movable property** (stocks, bonds, cash) can be created by a non-registered instrument or even orally — BUT always use a written, registered deed for enforceability
- Trust of **immovable property** (if applicable) MUST be created by a registered instrument

---

### Step 5: Stamp Duty Payment

**Timeline: Same day as execution**

Stamp duty varies by state. The trust deed must be **stamped BEFORE or ON the date of execution** (not after).

| State | Approximate Stamp Duty |
|-------|----------------------|
| **Maharashtra** | 2% of trust property value (capped at ₹25 lakhs) for revocable trusts |
| **Karnataka** | ₹500 (nominal for non-testamentary trust deeds) |
| **Delhi** | ₹100–₹500 (nominal) |
| **Tamil Nadu** | 1%–4% depending on property type |
| **Telangana** | Varies — check with sub-registrar |

> **Action**: Check with your state's sub-registrar office or use the [SHCIL e-stamping portal](https://www.shcilestamp.com/) for exact rates.

**Note**: For purely movable property (stocks, bonds, cash), many states charge a **nominal flat fee** rather than percentage-based duty. Confirm with your lawyer.

---

### Step 6: Register the Trust Deed

**Timeline: Week 5–6**

Although registration is technically optional for trusts of movable property under Section 5 of the Indian Trusts Act, **always register** for:
- Legal enforceability
- Opening bank/demat accounts in trust name
- Proof against future disputes

#### Registration Process

1. **Visit the Sub-Registrar's office** in the jurisdiction where the trust is being created
2. **Submit**:
   - Original trust deed on stamp paper (2 copies)
   - PAN cards of all parties
   - Aadhaar cards of all parties
   - Passport-size photos
   - Witness identity proofs
3. **Pay registration fee** (typically ₹1,000–₹30,000 depending on state and property value)
4. **Biometric verification** of settlor, trustee(s), and witnesses at the sub-registrar
5. Receive **registered trust deed** (typically within 7–15 working days)

**Deliverable**: Registered trust deed with registration number.

---

### Step 7: Obtain PAN for the Trust

**Timeline: Week 6–7**

Every trust must have its own PAN for income tax purposes.

1. **Apply for PAN** using Form 49A on the [NSDL/UTIITSL portal](https://www.onlineservices.nsdl.com/paam/endUserRegisterContact.html)
2. **Entity type**: Select "Trust"
3. **Documents required**:
   - Registered trust deed (certified copy)
   - PAN cards of all trustees
   - Address proof of trust (registered office address)
   - Passport photos of trustees
4. **Processing time**: 15–20 working days
5. **Cost**: ₹107 (including GST)

**Deliverable**: PAN card in the name of the trust.

---

### Step 8: Open Trust Bank Account

**Timeline: Week 7–8**

1. **Choose a bank** (most major banks — SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Kotak — support trust accounts)
2. **Submit**:
   - Registered trust deed
   - Trust PAN card
   - Resolution letter authorizing the trustee to open and operate the account
   - KYC documents of all trustees (PAN, Aadhaar, address proof, photos)
   - List of authorized signatories
3. **Account type**: Savings or current account in the name of the trust
   - Format: "[Trust Name] through Trustee [Your Name]"
4. **Initial deposit**: Transfer seed money from your personal account

**Deliverable**: Active bank account in trust name.

---

### Step 9: Open Trust Demat Account

**Timeline: Week 8–9**

To hold stocks and bonds in the trust's name, you need a demat account with a DP (Depository Participant).

1. **Choose a broker/DP** that supports trust accounts:
   - Zerodha, ICICI Direct, HDFC Securities, Kotak Securities all support trust demat accounts
   - **Zerodha**: Requires physical form submission for trust accounts (not online)
2. **Submit**:
   - Registered trust deed (notarized copy)
   - Trust PAN card
   - Board resolution / authorization letter (trustee(s) authorizing account opening)
   - KYC of all trustees
   - Bank proof (trust bank account statement or cancelled cheque)
   - SEBI-mandated KYC form for the trust entity
3. **Demat account** will be opened in: `[Trust Name] – [Trustee Name], Trustee`
4. **Trading account** (if needed): Same DP, linked to the demat account

**Important SEBI requirement**: Trust demat accounts are classified as **non-individual** accounts. Margin requirements and trading rules differ.

**Deliverable**: Active demat + trading account in trust name.

---

### Step 10: Transfer Assets into the Trust

**Timeline: Week 9–12**

#### A. Transfer Stocks (Demat Holdings)

1. **Submit an off-market transfer request** at your existing DP (personal demat)
2. **Fill DIS (Delivery Instruction Slip)** or use CDSL Easiest / NSDL SPEED-e:
   - **Transferor**: Your personal demat account
   - **Transferee**: Trust demat account
   - **Reason**: Transfer to self-settled trust (gift/settlement)
3. **Execute the transfer** for each ISIN
4. **Charges**: ₹25–₹30 per ISIN (DP charges) + GST
5. **Stamp duty on transfer**: 0.015% of market value (as per Indian Stamp Act amendment 2020 for off-market transfers)

> **Tax Note (Critical)**: Transfer of shares to a revocable trust where you are the settlor and beneficiary is generally **NOT treated as a transfer** under Section 47 of the Income Tax Act (no capital gains triggered), provided the trust is revocable and you are the beneficiary. However, **confirm with your CA** as this is a frequently litigated area.

#### B. Transfer Bonds / NCDs

- Same off-market DIS process as stocks if held in demat
- If held as physical certificates: execute a **deed of assignment** transferring to the trust, then dematerialize

#### C. Transfer Mutual Funds

1. **Submit a transmission form** to each AMC (Asset Management Company)
2. Attach: registered trust deed, trust PAN, KYC of trust
3. Units will be re-registered in the trust's name
4. Alternatively: redeem from personal folio and reinvest through trust folio (simpler but triggers capital gains)

#### D. Transfer Liquid Cash / Fixed Deposits

1. **Bank transfer** from personal account to trust bank account
2. For FDs: request bank to **close and re-book** in trust name, or transfer maturity proceeds
3. **Gift deed**: Execute a simple gift deed for cash transferred to the trust (establishes paper trail)

**Deliverable**: All assets reflected in trust demat account and trust bank account.

### Step 11: Income Tax Compliance

**Timeline: Ongoing annually**

#### Taxation of Revocable Trusts (Sections 60–64, Income Tax Act)

| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|--------------|
| **Revocable trust, settlor alive** | All income is **taxed in the hands of the settlor** at their slab rate (Section 61) |
| **Revocable trust, settlor deceased** | Trust becomes irrevocable by operation of law; taxed per Sections 161–164 |
| **Capital gains on transfer to trust** | Generally not a "transfer" if revocable and settlor is beneficiary (Section 47) |
| **Dividend income** | Taxed in settlor's hands |
| **Interest income** | Taxed in settlor's hands |

#### Annual Compliance

| Task | Deadline | Form |
|------|----------|------|
| **Trust ITR filing** | July 31 (or per extension) | ITR-5 or ITR-7 |
| **Settlor's ITR** | Include trust income in your personal ITR | Your usual ITR form |
| **Tax audit** | If trust income exceeds threshold (₹1 crore business / ₹50 lakh profession) | Form 3CA-3CD |
| **Trust PAN update** | If any trustee changes | NSDL/UTIITSL |

> **Key insight**: For a revocable trust, you essentially file **two returns** (trust + personal) but pay tax **only once** (in your personal return). The trust return is informational.

| **Deed amendments** | As needed | Add/remove beneficiaries, change trustees, modify distribution |

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Not registering the deed** — while legally optional for movable property, banks and DPs will refuse to open accounts without a registered deed
2. **Incorrect stamp duty** — understamping makes the deed inadmissible in court; overstamping wastes money
3. **No successor trustee** — if you're the sole trustee and become incapacitated, trust management freezes
4. **Mixing personal and trust funds** — maintain strict separation; commingling can pierce the trust
5. **Ignoring annual compliance** — missing trust ITR filing attracts penalties under Section 234F
6. **Assuming tax neutrality** — while revocable trusts are generally tax-transparent, specific transactions (e.g., transferring appreciated MFs) may trigger capital gains
7. **Not updating nominations** — demat/bank nominations should reflect the trust structure
8. **Verbal trusts** — legally valid under Section 5 for movable property but practically unenforceable; always use a written, registered deed
